01 / THE BUSINESS PROBLEM

Does the financial story reconcile?

A fictional service-business borrower has reported annual financial figures and submitted supporting tax information. The reviewer needs to identify variance between the two sources and describe what additional documentation could support a deeper credit evaluation.

    04 / REVIEW REASONING

    What I would document before escalation

    The default case uses simplified figures to make the reasoning visible while preserving the boundaries of a documentation review.

    1. Identify the revenue mismatch.

      Reported revenue is $1,200,000 while tax-document revenue is $1,120,000: an $80,000 difference, approximately 7.1% of tax-document revenue. I would request an explanation and supporting schedules.

    2. Show the arithmetic without overstating it.

      The simple $1,200,000 minus $900,000 expense calculation equals a $300,000 cash-flow proxy. Dividing by $210,000 of annual debt service gives approximately 1.43x. This is not lender-standard DSCR.

    3. List required evidence.

      A deeper review would need reconciled financial statements, tax return schedules, debt schedules, supporting accounting detail, and any adjustments required by the institution's methodology.

    4. State the limited recommendation.

      Document the source discrepancy and refer the reconciled package for authorized credit analysis. The illustration does not determine loan eligibility or recommend approval.

    RECRUITER TAKEAWAY

    Demonstrates variance analysis, careful use of financial ratios, supportable documentation requests, and clear boundaries between review and underwriting.

    What this demonstrates

    Examine differences between financial sources, calculate illustrative metrics, and explain what follow-up is needed before a formal credit determination.
